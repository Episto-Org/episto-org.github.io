About Episto

Errors compound when flawed papers are cited as if they were sound. Episto lets anyone check a reference list against retractions and against problems verified by a community of reviewers.

Where the flags come from

  • Official notices. Retractions, expressions of concern and corrections from the Retraction Watch database, which Crossref publishes openly. Imported every month.
  • Community flags. Problems that official notices do not cover: impossible numbers, failed replications, undisclosed conflicts of interest and more. See the flag guide.
  • Builds on flagged work. Computed from reference lists contributed to Episto: papers that cite work flagged Critical or Major.

How a community flag is decided

  1. A member proposes a flag with evidence that anyone can check.
  2. For 14 days, other members may ask about the evidence.
  3. A panel of reviewers is drawn by lottery from members in the paper's field. The draw uses a public random beacon (drand), so anyone can recompute it and nobody can steer it.
  4. Each reviewer judges the evidence alone and first posts only a sealed fingerprint of their verdict. When all have sealed, they reveal. Nobody sees another verdict before giving their own.
  5. The flag is published only if enough reviewers find that it holds.

Rounds, and why a flag cannot bounce back and forth

A flag can be disputed with new evidence. Each later round needs a larger panel and a larger majority to change the result, and after round 3 the case is settled. A settled case reopens only through an outside event: a retraction or correction, a published replication, or the authors releasing their data.

What is never flagged

Authors' motives or politics, disagreement with consensus, and people. See the full list.

For authors

If a flag concerns your work, you can send a response. It is linked from the paper's page, and a first response with new evidence can open a new round without waiting for the cooldown.

Privacy

This site sets no cookies and runs no analytics. The reference checker runs entirely in your browser; nothing you paste is sent anywhere. Reviewers are pseudonymous, and pages never name them.

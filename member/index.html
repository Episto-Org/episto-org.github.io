Members

Members propose flags and review them on panels drawn by lottery. Membership is by invitation, under a pseudonym: no name, email, address or phone number is asked for or kept.

How it works

  1. A member proposes a flag on a paper: one kind of problem, with evidence anyone can check.
  2. A panel is drawn by public lottery from members who know the method or the field. Nobody chooses who sits.
  3. Each reviewer judges alone and seals their review. All reviews open at once.
  4. The flag is listed if enough of the panel find it holds. Authors can respond, and their response is linked beside the flag.

No role decides a verdict, and flags judge the work, never the people. How flags are decided

Episto flags specific problems with specific research, decided by randomly drawn panels of pseudonymous reviewers. Flags are not judgements of people.
